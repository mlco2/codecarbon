# LLM Energy Benchmark — Specification v1

**Status:** Draft. Not yet implemented.
**Spec version string:** `1.0.0-draft`
**Record format:** a **profile of [BoAmps](https://github.com/Boavizta/BoAmps) 1.1.0** — see §5.
**Scope:** normative for `codecarbon benchmark` and for records accepted by the
`model_benchmarks` endpoint.

Keywords MUST / SHOULD / MAY are used in the RFC 2119 sense.

---

## 1. Purpose

Produce **reproducible, comparable measurements of IT energy per output token**
for a locally-served LLM, under fully stated conditions, in a form that can be
consumed as reference data by [EcoLogits](https://github.com/mlco2/ecologits).

A measurement produced under this spec answers exactly one question:

> On *this* hardware, with *this* engine, at *this* concurrency, how much IT
> energy does one output token of *this* model cost?

### Non-goals

- **Hosted APIs.** Impossible to measure; out of scope by construction.
- **Training.** Different workload shape, different spec.
- **Model quality.** This is an energy benchmark, not an eval harness.
- **Carbon.** Records carry energy only. Grid intensity, PUE, water and embodied
  impacts are applied downstream by the consumer. See §8.

### 1.1 Relationship to BoAmps

This spec does **not** define a new record format. BoAmps already models energy
reports for AI tasks, ships an LLM-inference example measured with CodeCarbon +
vLLM (`BoAmps/examples/energy-report-llm-inference.json`), and CodeCarbon
already has an exporter at `codecarbon/output_methods/boamps/`.

A benchmark record is therefore **a valid BoAmps report** that additionally
satisfies the constraints in §3, fills the required fields in §5.2, and carries
the `llmBenchmark` extension block in §5.3.

What this buys: BoAmps' hardware component model (better than a flat
`gpu_model`/`gpu_count` pair — it has per-component `manufacturer`, `family`,
`series`, `memorySize` and a `share` fraction for partially-allocated
hardware), its calibration-measurement fields, its measurement-provenance
fields, its validator (`BoAmps/tools/schema_validator/`), and interoperability
with the wider Boavizta ecosystem.

None of the BoAmps schemas set `additionalProperties: false`, so the extension
block in §5.3 is legal against the published schema. It SHOULD be proposed
upstream rather than kept as a private fork — see §9.2.

## 2. Definitions and units

| Term | Definition | BoAmps path |
|---|---|---|
| **Output token** | A token generated by the model and counted by the serving engine. Excludes prompt tokens. Under speculative decoding, counts *accepted* tokens only. | `task.dataset[dataUsage=output].dataQuantity` |
| **IT energy** | `cpu_energy + gpu_energy + ram_energy` as reported by CodeCarbon, in kWh, **before** any PUE multiplication. | `measures[].powerConsumption` |
| **Measurement window** | The interval between `start_task()` and `stop_task()`, covering steady-state generation only. | `measures[].measurementDuration` |
| **Concurrency** | Number of requests in flight simultaneously, as issued by the client. **Not** the engine's internal batch size — see §9.1. | `llmBenchmark.concurrency` (extension) |
| **Calibration measurement** | Whole-machine energy over a fixed interval with the model loaded but no requests in flight. BoAmps' term for what an idle baseline measures. | `measures[].powerCalibrationMeasurement` |

Energy is always **kWh**. Time is always **seconds**. Token counts are integers.

## 3. Measurement conditions (normative)

### 3.1 Energy MUST be reported PUE-free, with facility PUE recorded separately

`measures[].powerConsumption` MUST be raw IT energy as read from the hardware
counters, with **no PUE applied**. If the facility's real PUE is known, record it
in `llmBenchmark.facilityPue`; otherwise set it to `null`. Never fold it into
the energy figure.

CodeCarbon multiplies PUE into every hardware sample
(`codecarbon/emissions_tracker.py:1178`) and its BoAmps exporter maps the
already-multiplied `energy_consumed` straight into `powerConsumption`
(`output_methods/boamps/mapper.py:123`). **BoAmps has no PUE field at all**, so a
report produced this way is silently inflated with nothing recording it. Until
that is fixed upstream (§7.1), the harness MUST assert `tracker._pue == 1.0` and
refuse to emit otherwise.

> **Reviewer:** *"or maybe ecologits could ignore `datacenter_pue` when using
> data from codecarbon measures?"*
>
> **Resolved — the comment is right, and this section changed because of it.**
> The original text demanded PUE = 1.0 everywhere, which throws away real
> information: when someone benchmarks a self-hosted deployment in a facility
> whose PUE they actually know, that measured PUE is *better* than EcoLogits'
> generic per-provider constant (`tracers/utils.py:146`).
>
> The real defect is not which PUE wins — it is that a single pre-multiplied
> number leaves the consumer no choice at all. Separating the two fields lets
> EcoLogits decide per case:
>
> | Estimating for… | Rule |
> |---|---|
> | The same facility that was benchmarked, `facilityPue` known | Use `facilityPue`, ignore `datacenter_pue` — exactly as suggested |
> | Self-hosted, facility unknown | Use `datacenter_pue`, or 1.0 for on-desk hardware |
> | A hosted provider (§8.2 secondary use) | Use `datacenter_pue`; the measuring machine's facility is irrelevant |
>
> Requiring PUE-free energy is what makes all three reachable. Requiring
> PUE = 1.0 *at measurement time* is not the same thing, and was too strong.

### 3.2 The machine MUST be dedicated

No other significant workload during the window. The harness MUST take a
calibration measurement (§3.4) before and after the window and MUST abort if the
two differ by more than **10%**, which indicates background contamination.

`measures[].averageUtilizationGpu` SHOULD be ≥ 0.7 for a GPU-served benchmark.
A lower value means the measurement is dominated by something other than
generation, and the result is not a model energy figure.

### 3.3 The model MUST be fully resident before the window opens

Weights loaded into VRAM, CUDA graphs captured, allocator warm. Model load
energy MUST NOT fall inside the measurement window.

### 3.4 A calibration measurement MUST be recorded, and MUST NOT be subtracted

Measure ≥ 30 s with the model loaded and no requests in flight. Record as
`measures[].powerCalibrationMeasurement` (kWh) and
`measures[].durationCalibrationMeasurement` (s) — BoAmps defines these as
existing precisely "to isolate the initial consumption of the hardware".

Do **not** subtract it from `powerConsumption`.

*Rationale:* EcoLogits models the non-GPU part of the server explicitly
(`server_energy`, `impacts/llm.py:142`) and amortizes it across the batch.
Whole-machine energy including idle draw is the closer analogue to its
`request_it_energy` node. Recording the baseline separately lets a consumer
subtract it later; subtracting it at measurement time is irreversible.

### 3.5 Warmup requests MUST be discarded

Run ≥ **8** warmup requests at the target concurrency, outside the window.

### 3.6 Output length MUST be forced, not sampled

Set `max_tokens` to the target length **and disable early stopping**
(`ignore_eos` or engine equivalent). Otherwise output length varies with
sampler and prompt, and energy-per-token becomes workload-dependent rather
than model-dependent.

Sampling MUST be greedy (`temperature = 0`) or use a fixed seed.

### 3.7 Window size minimums

Energy counters (RAPL, NVML) are coarse and sampled on an interval. A window
MUST satisfy **both**:

- duration ≥ **120 s**
- total output tokens ≥ **5 000**

### 3.8 Repetitions

Each configuration MUST be measured in ≥ **3** independent windows **on the same
machine**. The record carries the **median**; the harness MUST reject the
configuration if the relative spread `(max - min) / median` exceeds **15%**.

This is a repeatability check, not a cross-machine one. See §10.

### 3.9 Prefix caching MUST NOT be able to serve a request

Engines with automatic prefix caching (enabled by default in recent vLLM)
detect a shared prompt prefix across requests and skip the input-processing
work entirely. A benchmark that reuses prompts would then measure the cache
rather than the model, and report an implausibly low figure.

Two independent defences, both required:

1. The harness MUST generate a **unique prefix per request** — the reference
   implementation prepends a per-request marker, so no two prompts share even
   their first tokens.
2. The operator SHOULD additionally disable prefix caching in the engine
   (`--no-enable-prefix-caching` for vLLM) and record it.

Defence 1 alone is sufficient in principle, but it depends on the tokenizer
splitting the marker as expected; disabling the feature removes the doubt.

### 3.10 Backends that cannot force output length

§3.6 is not satisfiable on every engine. Ollama's `num_predict` is an upper
bound only — there is no `ignore_eos` equivalent, so the model still stops at
EOS — whereas vLLM's `ignore_eos` plus `min_tokens` genuinely force the length.

A backend that cannot force output length MUST report
`forcedOutputLength: false`, which validity rule 5 rejects. Such runs remain
useful for local comparison but are not admissible as reference data. The
harness MUST NOT compensate by discarding short requests: their energy was
spent regardless, and dropping their tokens while keeping their energy inflates
the per-token figure.

## 4. Workload

### 4.1 Prompt set

A fixed, versioned prompt set ships with CodeCarbon at
`codecarbon/data/benchmark/prompts_v1.jsonl`. 64 prompts, natural English text,
no tool calls, no system prompt.

Prompts are tokenized per-model, so exact input lengths vary slightly. The
harness MUST record the **actual** measured input token total in
`task.dataset[dataUsage=input].dataQuantity`, not the target.

### 4.2 Configuration grid

| Axis | v1 values |
|---|---|
| Input length bucket | 128, 1024 tokens (nominal) |
| Output length | 256 tokens (fixed) |
| Concurrency | 1, 8, 32, 64 |

Every (input bucket × concurrency) pair is a **separate record**. Results MUST
NOT be averaged across concurrency levels — per-token energy varies by roughly
an order of magnitude between concurrency 1 and 64, and an averaged figure is
meaningless.

A submitter MAY run a subset of the grid. Concurrency 1 SHOULD always be
included as the comparability anchor.

## 5. Record format

### 5.1 Base

A valid BoAmps 1.1.0 report. Validate with `BoAmps/tools/schema_validator/`.

### 5.2 Profile constraints

Beyond BoAmps' own `required` lists, a benchmark record MUST set:

| Path | Constraint |
|---|---|
| `task.taskStage` | `"inference"` |
| `task.nbRequest` | count of measured (non-warmup) requests |
| `task.algorithms[0].algorithmType` | `"llm"` |
| `task.algorithms[0].foundationModelName` | present |
| `task.algorithms[0].foundationModelUri` | present for any publicly hosted model |
| `task.algorithms[0].parametersNumber` | billions, total |
| `task.algorithms[0].quantization` | one of `fp32`, `fp16`, `bf16`, `int8`, `int4` — **not** free text (the BoAmps example uses `"q16"` while the schema description suggests `"fp16"`; this profile pins the vocabulary) |
| `task.algorithms[0].framework`, `frameworkVersion` | serving engine and its version |
| `task.dataset[]` | exactly two entries, `dataUsage` `input` and `output`, both `dataType: "token"`, `dataQuantity` = **totals across the window**, not per-request |
| `measures[].measurementMethod` | `"codecarbon"` |
| `measures[].cpuTrackingMode` | `rapl` \| `constant` \| … — MUST be populated (see §7.1) |
| `measures[].gpuTrackingMode` | `nvml` \| … — MUST be populated when a `gpu` component is declared |
| `measures[].powerCalibrationMeasurement` / `durationCalibrationMeasurement` | §3.4 |
| `infrastructure.components[]` | one entry per `componentType` in {`cpu`, `gpu`, `ram`}; `share` MUST be set explicitly (1 for a dedicated machine) |
| `infrastructure.infraType` | required by BoAmps; matters here because cloud VMs have degraded measurement fidelity |
| `environment.country` | required by BoAmps |
| `header.publisher.confidentialityLevel` | `"public"` — a record that cannot be republished is not reference data |
| `header.licensing` | present; MUST permit redistribution in the EcoLogits snapshot |

### 5.3 The `llmBenchmark` extension

BoAmps has no vocabulary for the variables that dominate per-token energy. This
block carries them.

```json
"llmBenchmark": {
  "specVersion": "1.0.0-draft",
  "promptSet": "prompts_v1",

  "concurrency": 64,
  "reportedMeanBatchSize": 58.2,
  "outputTokensPerRequest": 256,
  "forcedOutputLength": true,
  "samplingMode": "greedy",
  "warmupRequests": 8,

  "modelRevision": "e0bc86c23ce5aae1db576c8cca6f06f1f73af2db",
  "activeParametersNumber": 7.25,
  "tensorParallelSize": 1,

  "deploymentId": "dfg-a100-box-1",
  "deploymentLabel": "Paris GPU box 1",

  "nWindows": 3,
  "relativeSpread": 0.041,

  "ttftP50S": 0.212,
  "tpsPerRequestP50": 41.6,

  "facilityPue": null,

  "energyBreakdownKwh": {
    "cpu": 0.00412,
    "gpu": 0.01187,
    "ram": 0.00061
  }
}
```

`energyBreakdownKwh` exists because BoAmps' `powerConsumption` is a single
scalar, and the CPU/GPU/RAM split is needed to sanity-check a record and to
support a future GPU-only mapping. The three values MUST sum to
`measures[].powerConsumption`.

`facilityPue` is `null` unless the submitter knows the real PUE of the facility
(§3.1). It is metadata about the *measuring* site, never a multiplier already
applied to the energy.

`deploymentId` and `deploymentLabel` are optional and submitter-chosen. They
name the deployment — a machine and its serving configuration — so a consumer
can select "my box" rather than "some A100". `deploymentId` is stable across
re-benchmarks of the same deployment; the record's own identity, which pins one
run, is the benchmark id assigned at ingestion.

Records of the same hardware class are expected to agree (§10, Gate 2), so
these fields are not required and should not be used to paper over
disagreements that indicate a missing field in §5. They exist for the case
where a submitter knows their two boxes genuinely differ.

### 5.4 Derived fields (server-side)

Computed by the server from the record; MUST NOT be accepted from clients,
otherwise the normalization cannot be recomputed when this spec changes.

```
it_energy_per_token = measures[0].powerConsumption
                      / task.dataset[dataUsage=output].dataQuantity   # kWh/token
latency_per_token_s = 1 / llmBenchmark.tpsPerRequestP50               # s/token
```

`it_energy_per_token` is the primary reference quantity. Everything else exists
to say under what conditions it holds.

## 6. Validity rules

Rejection is mechanical so it can run at ingestion.

1. Fails BoAmps schema validation, or fails any §5.2 constraint
2. `measurementDuration < 120` or output `dataQuantity < 5000` (§3.7)
3. `llmBenchmark.nWindows < 3` or `relativeSpread > 0.15` (§3.8)
4. `llmBenchmark.warmupRequests < 8` (§3.5)
5. `llmBenchmark.forcedOutputLength != true` (§3.6)
6. `energyBreakdownKwh.gpu == 0` while a `gpu` component is declared —
   indicates NVML unavailable rather than zero consumption
7. `energyBreakdownKwh` does not sum to `powerConsumption` (±1%)
8. `llmBenchmark.specVersion` unknown to the server
9. Any `infrastructure.components[].share != 1` — shared hardware is not
   measurable to this standard in v1
10. Implied mean power (`powerConsumption * 3.6e6 / measurementDuration`)
    outside **[calibration power, 2000 W]** — catches unit errors and counter
    wraparound
11. `averageUtilizationGpu < 0.7` when a `gpu` component is declared (§3.2)

Records passing these rules enter the store as `pending`. **Passing validation
is not approval.** Publication into the curated reference snapshot requires
human review (§8.3).

## 7. Implementation notes

### 7.1 Gaps in CodeCarbon's BoAmps exporter

The harness should reuse `map_emissions_to_boamps` rather than write a second
serializer. Three fixes are needed first, each useful upstream in its own right:

1. **`cpuTrackingMode` / `gpuTrackingMode` are omitted**
   (`mapper.py:116-119`), because `EmissionsData` does not carry them. They must
   be plumbed through from the hardware objects. This profile requires them —
   RAPL versus constant-power estimation is the difference between a measurement
   and a guess, and a curator has no way to tell them apart otherwise.
2. **`cpu_cores = cpu_count // 2`** (`mapper.py:166`) hardcodes 2-way SMT.
   Wrong on machines without hyperthreading and on 4-way SMT, so CPU
   `nbComponent` is unreliable as submitted.
3. **PUE is unrepresented** (§3.1). `powerConsumption` is silently
   PUE-multiplied with no field recording it. Propose a PUE field upstream to
   BoAmps; until then carry it in `llmBenchmark.facilityPue` and require
   PUE-free energy.

`mapper.py` also sets no `share` on generated components; this profile requires
it explicitly.

### 7.2 CLI surface

Implemented in `codecarbon/benchmark/`, exposed as:

```bash
codecarbon benchmark \
  --model mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 \
  --parameters 7.25 --quantization fp16 \
  --publisher "Data For Good" \
  --backend vllm \
  --concurrency 1,8,32,64 \
  --input-bucket 128,1024 \
  --out benchmark.boamps.json
```

The engine must already be serving the model; the command drives generation
against it rather than starting it. One BoAmps record is written per
(input bucket × concurrency) configuration, and each is checked against the §6
rules locally before it could ever be uploaded.

The harness owns the generation loop — it MUST NOT wrap an opaque user command,
because it needs authoritative output-token counts from the engine. Implemented
backends: `vllm` and any OpenAI-compatible `/v1/completions` server, plus
`ollama` (subject to §3.10). Window size is derived from throughput observed
during warmup so that both §3.7 minimums are met without the operator guessing
a request count.

Client and server run on the same machine, so client overhead is inside the
measurement. This is intentional and matches how the workload is actually
deployed, but it MUST be noted in the docs as a known inclusion.

## 8. Consumption by EcoLogits

### 8.1 Mapping

| Record field | EcoLogits node |
|---|---|
| `it_energy_per_token × output_token_count` | `request_it_energy` (`impacts/llm.py:184`) |
| `latency_per_token_s`, `ttftP50S` | `generation_latency` (`impacts/llm.py:65`) via `tps`/`ttft` |
| `infrastructure.components[gpu].nbComponent` | `gpu_required_count` (`impacts/llm.py:124`) |
| `llmBenchmark.facilityPue` | `datacenter_pue` (`impacts/llm.py:166`), per the table in §3.1 |

Overriding at `request_it_energy` — rather than at `gpu_energy` — short-circuits
the whole regression subtree in one place and avoids double-counting the
non-GPU contribution, which CodeCarbon's whole-machine measurement already
includes. Electricity mix, water and embodied impacts continue to be applied by
EcoLogits downstream, unchanged.

### 8.2 Applicability

A benchmark on a self-hosted deployment is **direct evidence about self-hosted
inference on comparable hardware**. It is not evidence about the same model
weights served by a commercial API on unknown hardware at unknown batch size.

- **Primary use:** a self-hosted / local path in EcoLogits, matched on
  (model, engine, hardware class, concurrency).
- **Secondary use:** offline recalibration of the regression coefficients
  `GPU_ENERGY_ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA` (`ecologits/impacts/llm.py:10-12`) from the
  corpus as a whole.

Runtime substitution of a measured value into a *hosted provider* estimate is
out of scope for v1.

### 8.3 Distribution

EcoLogits MUST NOT query the benchmark database at request time. Approved
records are aggregated into a versioned snapshot, reviewed, and shipped as
package data or fetched once at init with a disk cache.

## 9. Open questions

Unresolved. These need answers before v1 leaves draft.

1. **Continuous batching makes `concurrency` a proxy, not a control.** In vLLM
   and similar engines, the scheduler decides batch composition per step; the
   client controls only in-flight request count. `reportedMeanBatchSize` is
   recorded where the engine exposes it, but is not comparable across engines.
   Is client concurrency a stable enough axis?
2. **Should `llmBenchmark` be upstreamed to BoAmps as first-class fields?**
   Upstreaming is slower but avoids a de-facto fork of a format whose whole
   value is that everyone shares it. Recommend: implement behind
   `llmBenchmark`, open a BoAmps issue immediately, migrate if accepted. The
   PUE gap (§7.1.3) should go in the same issue.
3. **Multi-GPU accounting.** With tensor parallelism, `nbComponent` and
   `gpu_required_count` agree. With pipeline parallelism or replicas they
   diverge. v1 restricts to `tensorParallelSize == nbComponent`, single replica
   — too narrow to be useful?
4. **MoE token accounting.** `activeParametersNumber` is recorded, but energy
   per token depends on realized expert routing, which varies by prompt. Does
   the fixed prompt set control this adequately?
5. **Speculative decoding.** Accepted-token counting is defined, but acceptance
   rate is draft-model-dependent. Possibly exclude from v1.
6. **Prefill vs decode.** Two input buckets let a consumer infer the split but
   do not measure it. Worth a separate prefill-only window?
7. **Non-NVIDIA hardware.** AMD/ROCm, Apple Silicon and CPU-only inference have
   worse measurement fidelity. `gpuTrackingMode` lets consumers filter; does the
   profile need to reject low-fidelity sources outright?
8. **Anti-gaming.** Project tokens authenticate a project, not a submitter's
   honesty about their hardware. BoAmps' `header.publisher.publicKey` offers a
   signing hook — worth using, or over-engineering for the expected volume?
   §6 catches mechanical errors, not misattribution.
9. **`share < 1`.** Rule 6.9 rejects partially-allocated hardware, but BoAmps
   models `share` precisely because shared GPUs are common (MIG, vGPU, shared
   cloud instances). Is excluding them from v1 right, or does it exclude most
   of the people who would actually submit?

## 10. Validation gates before implementation

> **Reviewer:** *"why is that? 2 different machines should give different
> results."*
>
> **Correct — the original gate was stated wrong.** Two machines *should*
> disagree; energy per token is a property of model-and-hardware, not of the
> model alone. Rewritten below into three gates that test what actually
> matters.

The question a benchmark spec has to answer is not "do all machines agree" but
**"does the record capture everything that makes them disagree?"** If two
results differ and the recorded fields explain why, the spec works. If they
differ and nothing in the record accounts for it, something is uncontrolled and
the spec is incomplete.

### Gate 1 — Repeatability (same machine, same config)

Three or more windows, spread < 15% (§3.8). Failure means the measurement
procedure is noisy: window too short, warmup insufficient, or background load.

### Gate 2 — Reproducibility (different machines, *same hardware class*)

Two physically distinct machines with the same GPU model, engine version and
concurrency, ideally run by different people. Target agreement within **25%**.

This is the real test of the spec, and the one the original text was reaching
for. Same class of hardware and same declared config *should* converge; if two
A100-80GB boxes differ by 3×, some variable is real and unrecorded — thermal
throttling, PCIe versus SXM, host CPU, KV-cache configuration — and it needs a
field in §5 before v1 ships.

### Gate 3 — Explanatory power (different hardware classes)

Deliberately different machines — say A100 versus RTX 4090. Results will differ,
by a lot, and that is the expected outcome. The gate is qualitative: can a
reader looking only at the two records name the reason? If yes, the record is
self-describing and usable as reference data. If the difference has to be
explained from outside the record, add the missing field.

Gate 2 is the blocker for implementation. Gates 1 and 3 are cheap and should run
alongside it.
